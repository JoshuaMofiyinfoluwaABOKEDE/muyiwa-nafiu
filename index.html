The book

Serving As A Psalmist

Spiritual and ethical counsel for the gospel musician.

Written for every choir member, instrumentalist and worship leader serving somewhere between the altar and the choir stand, this is a plain-spoken handbook on character, calling and craft — on staying planted in a local church, handling pressure and pride, writing honest songs, and finishing the work faithfully, seen or unseen.

Resources

  • Leading Worship

    How to Lead a Song Session

    Leading a session is a sample of a larger flow, and it deserves careful planning. The call to worship, usually the first few words you speak before a song, should set the proper tone for the whole session. Some leaders resort to hypes and gingers, which are really tactics to get people to respond. You can be innovative, but be realistic about it. People do not receive their neighbour’s blessing in church by shouting louder hallelujahs.

    Read in Resources →
  • Heart of a Minister

    There’s No Small Work in God’s House

    ‘There is no little work of God.’ Whatever the Lord instructs is a big deal, even when it does not gather clout. Simple work is anything that does not make media waves, does not appeal to multitudes, and may not be the desire of an immature christian. The servant of the Lord should be content to run these errands, even if they earn no applause on this side of eternity.

    Read in Resources →
  • Calling & Growth

    The Three Stages of Every Gift

    The beginning should be the shortest stage in anyone’s life. At this stage a person’s movement is like that of the proverbial crab: the first few steps are a confusing sight, and you may not be able to tell its direction. You are a bundle of many gifts and much confusion. Serving in the church will help cut away the excesses and teach you focus and discipline.

    Read in Resources →
  • Songwriting

    Write to Establish, Not Just to Correct

    Avoid writing songs to correct a wrong notion. Write songs to establish a right notion instead, and do not come across as a negative person. Correction has its place, but the writer’s first task is usually to build up people, to plant a clear and true idea in their hearts that they can carry into the week ahead.

    Read in Resources →
  • Serving the Local Church

    Never Mess With the Flow

    You must know how to be blessed in a christian meeting from beginning to end, so that you can move with the flow instead of waiting for your turn to sing. If you always wait for your turn before you tune in, you will often miss the Lord. Every believers’ meeting is a chain of people edifying people by the Lord’s Spirit, and your session is not isolated from the others.

    Read in Resources →
  • Faith & Endurance

    Faith People — Choose Them Carefully

    Friendships matter. ‘I don’t know how to stay in touch with people’ is the doctrine of immature people. Keep your faith friendships and be loyal in them. Pray for your friends so that you do not become overfamiliar with them, and let them speak into your life even when their feedback is hard to hear.

    Read in Resources →
  • Leading Worship

    How to Introduce a New Song

    A worship session is not the place to introduce every song you have written. It is a congregational time of adoration, so the congregation should be considered when choosing songs. If a new song is to be done, let the lyrics be projected or printed so that people can relate to them quickly. The church should be helped to take part in the singing rather than left to watch the team perform.

    Read in Resources →
  • Heart of a Minister

    Serve From Sonship, Not Fear

    The sacred truth is that the One in whose presence you stand to lead His people in songs of praise, prayer and adoration is not an ordinary authority. He is your Father. That truth gives confidence: you are not a dog, and you are not a beggar. This is your Father’s house, and you belong here.

    Read in Resources →
  • Calling & Growth

    Hold Your Public Image Loosely

    Many people today measure honour by streams, ratings and reviews. These may come, and you can receive them with thanks, but they should never become the foundation of your joy. Your rating may not be favourable yet, and you may not have earned much recognition on streaming services. Work to improve with sincerity and holiness, and remind yourself often that recognition is not the goal.

    Read in Resources →
  • Songwriting

    Don’t Force Every Song Into the Same Shape

    Many songs will not obey the verse-chorus-verse-chorus-bridge rule. Avoid struggling to fit two verses, a chorus and a bridge into every song. That pattern may not always fit, especially with songs that come under the unction. Let the message guide the shape rather than forcing the message into a shape.

    Read in Resources →
  • Serving the Local Church

    Hidden Strengths in the Room

    In your church, if you pay attention to people, you will discover folks who are pivotal to the ongoing work but whose current duties may not yet make them noticeable. There are intercessors, prophetic people, and people on whom the Lord and the leadership have placed honour and authority. Some are in preparatory seasons of their callings. Others are meant to be unseen, yet their spiritual influence is heavy.

    Read in Resources →
  • Faith & Endurance

    Why You Need More Than One Income Stream

    Love for music alone is not a legitimate basis for going into full-time gospel ministry. It has to be a calling. If you love music, you can go into the music business, because loving something that brings no economic profit and does not pay your bills may cause frustration in the long term. As time unfolds, legitimate needs and demands will require urgent attention.

    Read in Resources →
  • Leading Worship

    Be Time-Conscious Onstage

    Many people use more time than necessary under the guise of ‘the songs keep coming’. If you are good at what you do, the songs will keep coming, but it is your job to put a lid on it when your time is up. Respecting the schedule is a form of care for the people who come after you and for the pastor who planned the programme.

About Muyiwa

A minister in word, in deed, and in song.

Muyiwa Nafiu — born Nafiu Olumuyiwa Kolade — grew up in Minna and now lives and serves in Ogbomoso, Nigeria. He studied Anatomy at LAUTECH, Ogbomoso, but ministry has carried most of his attention for years.

He serves on the Pastoral Council of True Worshippers Church, Ogbomoso, as Minister of Music under the pastoral grace of Rev. Dr. Niyi Adebayo. He also leads "O Timothy," a gathering where he trains gospel music ministers and worship leaders. He's married to Folasade Nafiu.

"I didn't become a worship leader or a gospel musician for a greener pasture."
